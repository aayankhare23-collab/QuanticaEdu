Algebra I · Lesson 2.2

Evaluating in Practice

Solve this lesson, free →All lessons

Evaluating was settled in 1.6, so nothing about the method changes here, the expressions just get heavier. Substitute in parentheses, compute by the order of operations, read off one number. This lesson is six problems, and each one adds a step the last did not have.

Problem
Evaluate \(5-4y\) at \(y=-7\). There are two minus signs in the second term, one printed in the expression and one that comes with the value, so write the substitution with parentheses to keep them separate.
Show a hint
  • Read \(4(-7)\) as one finished product before subtracting anything.
Show the full solution
The careless line reads \(5-28=-23\), which loses the value's sign. Substituted in parentheses, the term is \(4(-7)=-28\), and subtracting \(-28\) is adding \(28\), so \(5-4(-7)=5+28=\boxed{33}\).
Problem
Evaluate \(\dfrac{3k}{4}+\dfrac{20}{k}\) at \(k=8\). The variable sits under a fraction bar this time, and it appears in two places at once. Enter the value as a fraction in lowest terms.
Show a hint
  • Both \(k\)s become \(8\) at the same moment, one above a bar and one below, and each bar finishes as its own division.
Show the full solution
Combining across the bars into \(\frac{24+20}{4+8}=\frac{44}{12}\) merges two separate divisions and is wrong. Each bar finishes on its own, \(\frac{24}{4}=6\) and \(\frac{20}{8}=\frac{5}{2}\), so the value is \(6+\frac{5}{2}=\boxed{\dfrac{17}{2}}\).
Key idea
Same habits, heavier loads. Every problem here runs on the routine from 1.6, substitute in parentheses at every appearance, then work by the order from 1.2, writing each intermediate value down. What changes is where the variable sits. A letter can stand anywhere a number can, including under a fraction bar, and the bar still groups its whole top and its whole bottom. Writing the middle values down costs a few seconds and catches most sign errors before they compound.
Problem
Evaluate \(\sqrt{-12w}\) at \(w=-3\). The minus printed under the bar looks like the even-root trouble from 1.5. Substitute first, then decide whether the root exists.
Show a hint
  • Compute the number actually under the bar once \(w\) is replaced, then judge.
Show the full solution
Rejecting the root on sight is the tempting wrong turn, since the printed minus is not the value's sign. Substituting gives \(-12(-3)=36\) under the bar, and \(\sqrt{36}=\boxed{6}\).
Key idea
Judge after substituting. An even root needs a nonnegative value under its bar, the rule from 1.5, and the sign that matters is the sign of the value after substituting, never the sign printed in the expression. \(-12w\) opens with a minus on the page, yet it is positive whenever \(w\) is negative. This is the value-not-appearance idea from 1.1 working under a radical bar. Compute what sits under the bar first, and only then decide whether the root exists.
Problem
Evaluate \(n^2-5n+3\) at \(n=-2\). Three terms, three sign decisions, one value, and every appearance of \(n\) gets its own parentheses.
Show a hint
  • Write the parentheses at both appearances of \(n\) before doing any arithmetic.
  • Subtracting \(5(-2)\) is subtracting a negative.
Show the full solution
Reading the square as \(-2^2=-4\) gives a total of \(9\), and dropping the middle term's sign gives \(4-10+3=-3\), both wrong. Correctly \((-2)^2=4\) and \(-5(-2)=+10\), so the value is \(4+10+3=\boxed{17}\).
Problem
Evaluate \((2h-3)^2+h\) at \(h=6\). The parentheses hold a whole expression this time. Build the inside value first, square the result, then finish the sum.
Show a hint
  • The exponent applies to the whole parenthesized value, so finish \(2h-3\) before squaring anything.
Show the full solution
Squaring term by term gives \(144-9+6=141\), which is wrong because the exponent takes the finished inside value. That value is \(2(6)-3=9\), and \(9^2=81\), so the total is \(81+6=\boxed{87}\).
Key idea
Inside out with variables. A compound expression evaluates as a chain of small evaluations, innermost pieces first, which is the inside-out rule from 1.2 applied to substituted values. In \((2h-3)^2+h\), the inside finished as \(9\), the \(9\) fed the square, and the \(81\) fed the final sum. Each intermediate number is the input to the next layer, so a long expression is never one giant computation, it is many short ones in a row.
Problem
This one uses every skill from the lesson. Evaluate \(\dfrac{\sqrt{1-12v}}{v}\) at \(v=-4\). The bar covers all of \(1-12v\), the input is negative, and a division finishes the job. Enter the value as a fraction in lowest terms.
Show a hint
  • Substitute before touching the root, so compute \(1-12(-4)\) under the bar first.
  • After taking the root, divide by \(v\) itself. Its value is \(-4\), sign included.
Show the full solution
Two mistakes are tempting here. Stopping at the radical because of the minus sign in \(1-12v\) is one, but \(1-12(-4)=1+48=49\), which is fine under the bar. Dropping the sign of the denominator is the other, and it gives \(\dfrac{7}{4}\), also wrong. The top is \(\sqrt{49}=7\), the bottom is \(-4\), and \(7\div(-4)=\boxed{-\dfrac{7}{4}}\).
Fun fact
A GPS receiver finds your distance from each satellite by evaluating a compound expression, squared differences feeding a square root, inside out, many times every second.

The expressions got heavier and the method never changed. Parentheses on every substitution, signs judged after substituting, computation run from the inside out. Next, 2.3 turns the distributive property around, reading \(ab+ac\) as \(a(b+c)\), a rewrite called factoring.

Practice these ideas

Practice
Evaluate \(9-2t\) at \(t=-3\).
Show the solution
Dropping the sign reads the second term as \(2\cdot 3\) and gives \(9-6=3\). With parentheses the substitution is \(9-2(-3)=9+6=\boxed{15}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\dfrac{m}{3}+m\) at \(m=12\).
Show the solution
Pulling the lone \(m\) under the bar reads \(\frac{12+12}{3}=8\), but the bar groups only its own top. The first term is \(12\div 3=4\), and \(4+12=\boxed{16}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\sqrt{2-7d}\) at \(d=-2\). Substitute before judging the root.
Show the solution
Rejecting at sight because of the printed \(-7d\) is the tempting wrong turn, but the sign that matters is the value's after substituting. Since \(-7(-2)=14\), the bar holds \(2+14=16\), and \(\sqrt{16}=\boxed{4}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(w^2+3w\) at \(w=-18\).
Show the solution
Reading the square as \(-18^2=-324\) makes the total \(-378\). With parentheses, \((-18)^2=324\) and \(3(-18)=-54\), so the value is \(324-54=\boxed{270}\).
Practice
Evaluate \((3g-4)^2\) at \(g=5\).
Show the solution
Squaring \(g\) first reads \(3(25)-4=71\), but the exponent applies to the whole parenthesized value. Inside first, \(3(5)-4=11\), and \(11^2=\boxed{121}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\dfrac{40}{p}-p\) at \(p=-5\).
Show the solution
The tempting slip is to drop the negative in the division, read \(40\div 5=8\), and land on \(8+5=13\). The first term is really \(40\div(-5)=-8\), and subtracting \(p\) means subtracting \(-5\), so \(-8+5=\boxed{-3}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\sqrt{h^2+63}\) at \(h=-1\).
Show the solution
Reading the square as \(-1^2=-1\) leaves \(62\) under the bar, which has no clean root. Correctly \((-1)^2=1\), so the bar holds \(1+63=64\), and \(\sqrt{64}=\boxed{8}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\dfrac{a^2+b}{a+b}\) at \(a=-3\) and \(b=6\).
Show the solution
Reading \(a^2\) as \(-3^2=-9\) makes the top \(-3\) and the value \(-1\). With parentheses the top is \((-3)^2+6=15\) over a bottom of \(-3+6=3\), so the value is \(15\div 3=\boxed{5}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(2c^2-c^3\) at \(c=-3\).
Show the solution
Treating the cube as \(+27\) gives \(18-27=-9\). The even power clears the sign, so \(2(-3)^2=18\), while the odd power keeps it, so \((-3)^3=-27\), and \(18-(-27)=18+27=\boxed{45}\).
Practice
Evaluate \(\dfrac{\sqrt{50-t^2}}{t+12}\) at \(t=-5\). Enter the value as a fraction in lowest terms.
Show the solution
Reading \((-5)^2\) as \(-25\) puts \(75\) under the bar with no clean root, and reading the bottom as \(-5-12\) breaks the other half. The bar holds \(50-25=25\), so the top is \(5\), the bottom is \(-5+12=7\), and the value is \(\boxed{\dfrac{5}{7}}\).
