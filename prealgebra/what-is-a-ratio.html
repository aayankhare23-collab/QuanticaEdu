Prealgebra · Lesson 7.1

What Is a Ratio?

Solve this lesson, free →All lessons

A smoothie owner keeps the flavor steady by using the same comparison every time, three mango chunks for every two yogurt scoops. That balance between two quantities is a ratio, and it holds whether she is making one cup or filling a pitcher.

Problem
Each scoop drops 5 almonds and 3 dried cherries into a bag. A customer orders 4 scoops. How many dried cherries land in the bag?
Show a hint
  • Every scoop is identical, and the cherries from one scoop do not depend on the almonds at all. Just count the cherries one scoop brings, then ask how many scoops there are.
Show the full solution
The almonds are along for the ride here. What matters is that every scoop is identical, so the cherries simply repeat, three of them at a time. One scoop brings \(3\) cherries. Four scoops bring four copies of that same group, which is \(4\) groups of \(3\). So the count is \(4 \times 3 = 12\). Notice we never had to touch the almonds to answer this, the cherry side of the pairing carries its own steady count. The bag holds \(\boxed{12}\) dried cherries.
Problem
At a kite festival the rule is 3 yellow kites for every 5 red ones. One cluster has 9 yellow kites. How many red kites are in the cluster?
Show a hint
  • The phrase for every 3 yellow there are 5 red is a ratio of \(3:5\). The yellow part grew from \(3\) to \(9\). How many times bigger is that? That same multiplier has to act on the red part too, or the comparison would change.
  • To go from \(3\) yellow to \(9\) yellow you multiply by \(3\), since \(3\times 3 = 9\). Apply that same \(\times 3\) to the \(5\) reds.
Show the full solution
The rule for every \(3\) yellow there are \(5\) red is the ratio \(3:5\), and a ratio works much like a fraction in simplest form. As long as you scale both parts by the same number, the comparison stays exactly the same. The yellow part has grown from \(3\) to \(9\). To find the multiplier, ask what \(3\) was multiplied by to reach \(9\). Since \(3\times 3 = 9\), the multiplier is \(3\), which means every part is tripled. Now apply that same \(\times 3\) to the red part, because both colors rise together. That gives \(5\times 3 = 15\) red kites. You can sanity check it. The scaled comparison \(9:15\) reduces back to \(3:5\), so the ratio held steady. \(\boxed{15}\)
Key idea
A ratio compares two amounts by their relative size. Writing $3:2$ means for every $3$ of the first there are $2$ of the second, and that pairing holds no matter how big the batch grows. The same comparison can be written three ways: $3:2$ with a colon, "3 to 2" in words, or the fraction $\tfrac{3}{2}$, and the colon form is the one you will reach for most. Order carries meaning, so mango to yogurt is a different comparison than yogurt to mango.
Problem
A garden plants basil and mint in the ratio \(4:5\). One full repeat of the pair uses \(4+5=9\) plants, so every valid total is a multiple of \(9\). What is the only possible total between 30 and 40?
Show a hint
  • One full repeat of the comparison is \(4\) basil and \(5\) mint, so each bundle is \(4+5=9\) plants. The real total has to be made of whole bundles, so it must be a multiple of \(9\).
  • List the multiples of \(9\) and find the one that lands between \(30\) and \(40\). You have \(9, 18, 27, 36, 45\). Which single one fits?
Show the full solution
Each full repeat of the ratio brings \(4\) basil and \(5\) mint, a bundle of \(4+5=9\) plants. Since the garden is built from whole bundles, the total number of plants must be a multiple of \(9\). The multiples of \(9\) are \(9, 18, 27, 36, 45,\) and so on. The only one that sits between \(30\) and \(40\) is \(36\), which is exactly \(4\) bundles. Check it. Four bundles give \(4\times 4=16\) basil and \(4\times 5=20\) mint, and \(16+20=36\), still in the comparison \(4:5\). So the only possible total is \(\boxed{36}\).
Key idea
One ratio fits a whole family of totals. If the comparison is $a:b$, the real counts could be $a$ and $b$, or twice that, or any matched scale-up, so a ratio alone never says how many things there are in all. What it does pin down is the size of one full repeat, $a+b$, which is why every honest total lands on a whole number of those repeats. To turn a ratio into actual amounts you need one extra fact: the total, or one of the two quantities.
Problem
A board game ships tokens in the ratio \(18:24\). Reduce it to lowest terms. Write your answer as a:b.
Show a hint
  • List the factors that \(18\) and \(24\) share. Both are even, so \(2\) works, and both are divisible by \(3\), so \(6\) divides each. Is there anything bigger than \(6\) that divides both?
  • The greatest common factor is \(6\). Divide each part by \(6\): \(18\div 6\) gives the new first number and \(24\div 6\) gives the new second number. Then check the two results share no common factor larger than \(1\).
Show the full solution
Treat the ratio like a fraction you want in simplest form. First find the greatest common factor of the two parts. The factors of \(18\) are \(1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18\), and the factors of \(24\) are \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24\). The largest number in both lists is \(6\), so the greatest common factor is \(6\). Now divide each part by \(6\). For the red tokens, \(18\div 6 = 3\). For the blue tokens, \(24\div 6 = 4\). That turns \(18:24\) into \(3:4\). Finally, check that you are truly finished. The only common factor of \(3\) and \(4\) is \(1\), so nothing larger divides both and the ratio cannot shrink any further. The simplest form of the comparison is \(\boxed{3:4}\).
Problem
A school library returned books on time to late in the ratio \(45:75\). Reduce to simplest form. Write as a:b.
Show a hint
  • Simplifying a ratio works the same way as simplifying a fraction. You want the greatest common factor of \(45\) and \(75\), the biggest number that divides both cleanly. Both end in a \(5\) or a \(0\), so \(5\) divides each, but check whether something larger does too.
  • Both numbers are multiples of \(15\), since \(45 = 3 \times 15\) and \(75 = 5 \times 15\). Divide each part of the ratio by \(15\), then check that the two results have no common factor left bigger than \(1\).
Show the full solution
Find the greatest common factor of \(45\) and \(75\), the largest number that divides both with nothing left over. It is tempting to stop at \(5\) since both numbers end in a \(5\), but you can do better. List what fits into each. The number \(45\) breaks into \(3 \times 3 \times 5\), and \(75\) breaks into \(3 \times 5 \times 5\). They share one \(3\) and one \(5\), so their greatest common factor is \(3 \times 5 = 15\). Now divide both parts of the ratio by \(15\). On time becomes \(45 \div 15 = 3\), and late becomes \(75 \div 15 = 5\). That gives \(3:5\). Finally, confirm it is fully reduced. The only number that divides both \(3\) and \(5\) is \(1\), so there is nothing left to cancel. The library's month came down to a clean \(\boxed{3:5}\), three on time for every five late.
Problem
A cooling fluid should compare water to coolant as \(2:3\). Six ratios were recorded: \(4:6\), \(8:10\), \(6:9\), \(9:12\), \(10:15\), \(14:21\). How many are equivalent to \(2:3\)?
Show a hint
  • Test each ratio by dividing both numbers by their greatest common factor, the same move you used to reduce a fraction to lowest terms. Then see whether what is left is \(2:3\).
  • Try \(4:6\). Both numbers divide by \(2\), giving \(2:3\), so that one matches. Now do the same simplifying step to \(8:10\) and notice it becomes \(4:5\), which is not \(2:3\). Work through all six and keep a running count of the ones that land on \(2:3\).
Show the full solution
Reduce each ratio to its simplest form and compare it with \(2:3\). For \(4:6\), both numbers divide by \(2\), giving \(2:3\). Match. For \(8:10\), both divide by \(2\), giving \(4:5\). Not a match. For \(6:9\), both divide by \(3\), giving \(2:3\). Match. For \(9:12\), both divide by \(3\), giving \(3:4\). Not a match. For \(10:15\), both divide by \(5\), giving \(2:3\). Match. For \(14:21\), both divide by \(7\), giving \(2:3\). Match. The ratios \(4:6\), \(6:9\), \(10:15\), and \(14:21\) all reduce to \(2:3\), while \(8:10\) and \(9:12\) do not. That gives \(\boxed{4}\) matching ratios.
Problem
A trail map records two legs as \(0.75\,\text{km}\) and \(1.25\,\text{km}\). Write \(0.75:1.25\) as a whole-number ratio in lowest terms, in the form a:b.
Show a hint
  • The decimals reach the hundredths place, so multiply both parts by \(100\). That turns \(0.75\) into \(75\) and \(1.25\) into \(125\), giving the whole-number ratio \(75:125\). Stretching both legs by the same factor of \(100\) keeps the comparison identical, the same way \(\tfrac{75}{125}\) and \(\tfrac{0.75}{1.25}\) are equal fractions.
  • Now reduce \(75:125\) the way you reduced fractions in Chapter 4. The largest number that divides both \(75\) and \(125\) is \(25\). Divide each part by \(25\) and see what whole-number ratio is left.
Show the full solution
Start with the decimal ratio \(0.75:1.25\). Both numbers stop at the hundredths place, so multiplying each part by \(100\) clears the decimals. $$0.75:1.25 = (0.75\times 100):(1.25\times 100) = 75:125$$ Multiplying both parts by the same number does not change the comparison, just like scaling the top and bottom of a fraction by \(100\) leaves its value alone, so \(75:125\) describes the same trail. Now reduce \(75:125\) to lowest terms. The greatest common factor of \(75\) and \(125\) is \(25\), so divide each part by \(25\). $$75:125 = (75\div 25):(125\div 25) = 3:5$$ Since \(3\) and \(5\) share no factor larger than \(1\), the ratio is fully reduced. The two legs compare as \(\boxed{3:5}\).
Problem
A bakery uses \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) cup almond flour and \(\tfrac{4}{9}\) cup tapioca starch. Write the ratio in lowest terms as a:b.
Show a hint
  • Both fractions sit over a denominator that divides into \(9\). Multiply each part of the ratio by \(9\). Multiplying both parts by the same number gives an equivalent ratio, the same way \(\tfrac{6}{4}\) and \(\tfrac{3}{2}\) name the same fraction.
  • After scaling you get \(6:4\). Now finish like you would simplify a fraction. Divide both parts by the largest number that goes into each.
Show the full solution
Start with the ratio of almond flour to tapioca starch, which is \(\tfrac{2}{3}:\tfrac{4}{9}\). Fractions make a ratio hard to read, so clear them first. Multiplying both parts of a ratio by the same number gives an equivalent ratio, and the number \(9\) works for both denominators. $$\tfrac{2}{3}\times 9 = 6 \qquad \tfrac{4}{9}\times 9 = 4$$ So \(\tfrac{2}{3}:\tfrac{4}{9}\) is the same comparison as \(6:4\). Now reduce, exactly like putting a fraction in simplest form. Both parts share a common factor of \(2\), so divide each part by \(2\). $$6\div 2 = 3 \qquad 4\div 2 = 2$$ That leaves \(3:2\), and since \(3\) and \(2\) share no common factor larger than \(1\), it is fully reduced. For every \(3\) parts of almond flour the blend has \(2\) parts of tapioca starch. \(\boxed{3:2}\)
Key idea
Equivalent ratios and simplest form. A ratio acts like a fraction. Multiply both parts of $a:b$ by the same number for an equivalent ratio (the same comparison in bigger numbers, like $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and $\tfrac{6}{8}$), or divide both parts by a shared factor to shrink it. Dividing by the greatest common factor as far as it goes gives simplest form, reached when the two parts share no common factor larger than $1$. If a ratio has fractions, first multiply every part by a common denominator. If it has decimals, multiply by a power of ten. Then reduce. Clear first, then reduce.
Fun fact
A screen\'s aspect ratio is width to height: \(16:9\) for modern TVs, \(4:3\) for older monitors. Scaling \(4:3\) to \(16:12\) shows \(16:9\) is shorter and wider. One ratio covers every screen size.

Turn a ratio into a count. So far a ratio has fixed only the relative sizes, never the totals. The move that unlocks real amounts is to read a ratio as a recipe built from equal parts, \(a\) parts of one thing and \(b\) parts of the other, every part the same size. Once you find what a single part is worth, every quantity in the story drops out by simple multiplication.

Problem
A bag holds 56 pieces of dried fruit mixed as raisins to pumpkin seeds in the ratio \(5:3\). How many pumpkin seeds are in the bag?
Show a hint
  • The ratio \(5:3\) means the bag is built from \(5\) equal parts of raisins and \(3\) equal parts of seeds. Add those up to see how many equal parts make the whole bag.
  • There are \(5+3=8\) equal parts in all, and they must total \(56\) pieces. So one part is \(56\div 8\). The seeds are the \(3\) parts, so multiply that one part value by \(3\).
Show the full solution
The ratio \(5:3\) tells you the bag is made of \(5\) equal parts of raisins and \(3\) equal parts of pumpkin seeds. Notice that the ratio by itself does not tell you the totals, it only tells you how the pieces share out. To bring in the real count, add the parts. There are \(5+3=8\) equal parts, and all \(8\) of them together must add up to the whole bag of \(56\) pieces. So each part is worth $$56 \div 8 = 7 \text{ pieces.}$$ Now scale each side by that value. The raisins are \(5\) parts, which is \(5\times 7 = 35\) pieces, and the pumpkin seeds are \(3\) parts, which is \(3\times 7 = 21\) pieces. As a quick check, \(35+21=56\), exactly the size of the bag, and \(35:21\) reduces back to \(5:3\). The bag holds \(\boxed{21}\) pumpkin seeds.
Key idea
The parts method. Read $a:b$ as a pile of $a+b$ equal parts. If you know the total, divide it by $a+b$ to find what one part is worth, then give $a$ of those parts to one side and $b$ to the other. If instead you know one quantity, divide it by its part count to find one part, then scale the other side by that same multiplier. Once you know what one part is, every amount follows.
Problem
A box of 84 stickers is split between two friends in the ratio \(5:7\), bigger share to the friend who paid more. How many stickers are in the smaller share?
Show a hint
  • Add the two numbers in the ratio to find how many equal parts the box is divided into. Then figure out how many stickers sit in a single part.
  • One part is \(84 \div 12 = 7\) stickers. The smaller share is the \(5\)-part side, so it holds five of those parts.
Show the full solution
The ratio \(5:7\) tells you the box is shared in \(5 + 7 = 12\) equal parts. To find the size of one part, divide the total by the number of parts, so \(84 \div 12 = 7\) stickers per part. The smaller share is the \(5\)-part side, which is \(5 \times 7 = 35\) stickers. As a quick check, the larger share is \(7 \times 7 = 49\), and \(35 + 49 = 84\), the whole box. The smaller share holds \(\boxed{35}\) stickers.
Problem
A lemonade recipe uses lemon juice to water in the ratio \(2:9\). One batch uses 6 ounces of lemon juice. How many ounces does the whole batch hold?
Show a hint
  • The lemon side of the ratio is \(2\) parts, and those \(2\) parts landed on six ounces. What number do you multiply \(2\) by to get \(6\)? That single number is the multiplier for every part of the ratio.
  • One full repeat of the recipe is \(2 + 9 = 11\) parts. Multiply those \(11\) parts by the multiplier you found, and you have the whole batch in ounces.
Show the full solution
Start from the side you actually know. The lemon juice is \(2\) parts in the ratio, and in this batch those \(2\) parts come out to six ounces. So each single part must be worth \(6 \div 2 = 3\) ounces. That number \(3\) is the multiplier, the amount one part is really worth. Now scale the whole recipe by it. One complete repeat of the ratio is \(2 + 9 = 11\) parts, so the finished batch is \(11 \times 3 = 33\) ounces. You can check it by parts. The lemon juice is \(2 \times 3 = 6\) ounces, the water is \(9 \times 3 = 27\) ounces, and \(6 + 27 = 33\) ounces, which matches. The whole batch is \(\boxed{33}\) ounces.
Problem
An aquarium opens with 40 fish, blue to green in \(3:2\). The keeper adds only green fish until the ratio reads \(6:5\). How many green fish must the keeper add?
Show a hint
  • The blue total never moves, it stays at \(24\). So your job is to find how much green pairs with \(24\) blue when the comparison is \(6:5\), then compare that to the \(16\) green already swimming there.
  • In the new comparison \(6:5\), the blue is \(6\) parts and equals \(24\) fish, so one part is \(24\div 6=4\). The green is \(5\) parts. Find the green count, then subtract the \(16\) you started with.
Show the full solution
Start by turning the opening ratio into real counts. The ratio \(3:2\) means \(3+2=5\) parts, and \(40\div 5=8\) fish per part, so there are \(3\times 8=24\) blue and \(2\times 8=16\) green. Only green fish get added, so the blue stays anchored at \(24\) forever. Now read the target comparison \(6:5\) the same way. Blue is the \(6\) side and it equals \(24\) fish, so one part is \(24\div 6=4\) fish. The green side is \(5\) parts, which is \(5\times 4=20\) green fish. The tank already holds \(16\) green, so the keeper needs \(20-16=4\) more. Check it, with \(24\) blue and \(20\) green the comparison is \(24:20\), and dividing both by \(4\) gives \(6:5\), exactly the goal. So the keeper must add \(\boxed{4}\) green fish.

A vendor packs gift bags with 4 glitter stickers and 6 mini erasers each. She fills 5 bags. How many mini erasers does she use in total?
Show the solution
A ratio inside one bag pairs the stickers and erasers, but this question asks only about the erasers, so the sticker count is a distraction. Each bag holds the same six mini erasers, and she fills five identical bags. Five bags each carrying six erasers gives \(5 \times 6 = 30\) erasers in all. So she uses \(\boxed{30}\) mini erasers across the five bags.

A swim club keeps 2 coaches for every 9 swimmers. This morning 36 swimmers arrived. How many coaches are on deck?
Show the solution
The staffing is fixed at \(2:9\), two coaches for every nine swimmers. To keep the comparison the same when more people show up, both parts of the ratio scale by the same multiplier. The swimmers went from \(9\) to \(36\), and \(36 \div 9 = 4\), so the for every unit repeats four times. The coaches must repeat four times as well, which gives \(2 \times 4 = 8\) coaches. As a check, \(8:36\) reduces by dividing both parts by \(4\), and \(8 \div 4 = 2\) with \(36 \div 4 = 9\), so \(8:36\) is the same comparison as \(2:9\). There are \(\boxed{8}\) coaches on deck.

A fabric border repeats wide stripes to narrow stripes in the ratio \(9:12\). Reduce first, then scale up so the wide count reaches 24. How many narrow stripes go with 24 wide ones?
Show the solution
Start by reducing the comparison. Both parts of \(9:12\) share a common factor of \(3\), so dividing each part gives \(\tfrac{9}{3}:\tfrac{12}{3}\), which is \(3:4\). That tells you the simplest pattern is \(3\) wide stripes for every \(4\) narrow ones. Now scale up to reach \(24\) wide stripes. To turn the \(3\) wide stripes into \(24\), multiply by \(8\), since \(3\times 8 = 24\). To keep the comparison the same, multiply the narrow part by that same factor, so \(4\times 8 = 32\). You can check it against the original numbers too. Going from \(9\) wide to \(24\) wide is a factor of \(\tfrac{24}{9} = \tfrac{8}{3}\), and \(12\times \tfrac{8}{3} = 32\) as well. Either path lands in the same place. So \(24\) wide stripes go with \(\boxed{32}\) narrow stripes.

A juice bar restocks mango and guava pods in the ratio \(24:36\). Simplify to lowest terms. Write your answer as a:b.
Show the solution
A ratio behaves like a fraction, so simplifying it works the same way you reduced fractions in Chapter 4. You divide both parts by the same number until nothing is left to cancel. The fastest route is to divide by the greatest common factor of the two parts at once. The greatest common factor of \(24\) and \(36\) is \(12\), since \(12\) divides both and no larger number does. The only positive factor shared by \(2\) and \(3\) is \(1\), so there is nothing left to cancel and the ratio is in lowest terms. The simplified ratio is \(\boxed{2:3}\).

The two amounts being compared are \(\tfrac{5}{6}\) cup of berries and \(\tfrac{5}{9}\) cup of banana, so the ratio is \(\tfrac{5}{6}:\tfrac{5}{9}\). Show the solution
The ratio \(4:5\) tells you copper takes \(4\) parts and jade takes \(5\) parts, so the bracelet is divided into \(4+5=9\) equal parts. Show the solution
The ratio \(3:8\) means compost takes \(3\) parts and sand takes \(8\) parts, so one full repeat of the mix is \(3 + 8 = 11\) kilograms. The compost is the \(3\) part, and here it actually weighs \(15\) kilograms. To find the multiplier, ask what \(3\) is multiplied by to give \(15\). One repeat weighs \(11\) kilograms, so the full batch is \(11 \times 5 = 55\) kilograms. You can check it by parts. Compost is \(3 \times 5 = 15\) kilograms and sand is \(8 \times 5 = 40\) kilograms, and \(15 + 40 = 55\) kilograms. The total mass of the batch is \(\boxed{55}\).
Practice
A reef tank holds 35 fish in the ratio \(3:4\) blue to gold. The keeper adds only blue fish until the count is \(1:1\). How many blue fish must she add?
Show the solution
The ratio \(3:4\) divides the tank into \(3+4=7\) equal parts. With \(35\) fish in all, one part is \(35\div 7=5\) fish. So the tank starts with \(3\times 5=15\) blue fish and \(4\times 5=20\) gold fish, which checks out since \(15+20=35\). Now only blue fish get added, so the gold count stays locked at \(20\). An even split of \(1:1\) means blue must climb until it matches the gold, so blue needs to reach \(20\). It began at \(15\), so the keeper adds \(20-15=5\) blue fish. \(\boxed{5}\)
