Four operations, but underneath them only two. Addition and multiplication are the genuine ones, each carrying the same freedoms of order and grouping. Subtraction and division are those two seen in a mirror, reached through the opposite and the reciprocal. Here is the whole chapter on a single page, section by section.

Key idea
Subtraction is adding the opposite, $a-b=a+(-b)$, and division is multiplying by the reciprocal, $a\div b=a\times\frac1b$. So the four operations are really two. The order and grouping freedoms of $+$ and $\times$, together with the distributive law $a(b+c)=ab+ac$ that links them, are the bedrock every later trick stands on.
Key idea
When operations mix, work in this order. Parentheses, then exponents, then $\times$ and $\div$ from left to right, then $+$ and $-$ from left to right.

That is the whole toolkit of Chapter 1. Everything ahead, exponents, primes, fractions, and the algebra past them, is built on these same rules.
