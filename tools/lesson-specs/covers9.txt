9.1 Meet the Quadratic. Distributing twice to expand a product of two binomials, and the
forward pattern (x+r)(x+s) = x^2+(r+s)x+rs. The three named identities, (a+b)^2, (a-b)^2 and
the difference of squares (a+b)(a-b) = a^2-b^2. The zero product property, and the fact that a
quadratic has at most two solutions because each linear factor is zero at one number.

9.2 Factoring x^2+bx+c. The search for two numbers with r+s=b and rs=c, and the sign reasoning
that narrows it, a positive c means r and s share a sign while a negative c means they differ,
with the sign of b naming which is farther from zero. Pulling out a common factor first, and
pulling out x itself when there is no constant term. The difference of squares as a shortcut.
The honest limit, that some quadratics do not factor over the integers. The full pipeline,
rearrange to one side zero, factor, then set each factor to zero.

9.3 Factoring ax^2+bx+c. Reading the three conditions off (px+q)(rx+s) = prx^2+(ps+qr)x+qs, so
only binomials whose leading terms multiply to ax^2 and whose constants multiply to c are worth
trying. The organized search over the factor pairs of a and of c, narrowed by the size and
parity of b. Common factor first, then the identity shapes, before any list is written. A
factor px+q gives the root -q/p, so roots here are often fractions. The extended pipeline.

9.4 Roots, Sums, and Products. For x^2+bx+c the roots sum to -b and multiply to c, derived by
matching (x-r)(x-s) = x^2-(r+s)x+rs against the quadratic rather than asserted. The general
case, r+s = -b/a and rs = c/a, reached by dividing through by a. Building the monic quadratic
x^2-(sum)x+(product) from a stated sum and product. Answering questions about the roots WITHOUT
finding them, using the symmetric rewritings 1/r+1/s = (r+s)/(rs), r^2+s^2 = (r+s)^2-2rs,
(r-s)^2 = (r+s)^2-4rs and r^2s+rs^2 = rs(r+s). Finding a missing coefficient from a stated fact
about the roots, such as a fixed ratio, a stated difference, or equal roots. Finding the second
root from the first in one step. The sign trap, that the sum is -b and not b, taught and tested.
The honest limit, that the two facts on their own never produce the roots and that some stated
sum and product pairs are not real at all.

9.5 Factoring in Action. An equation does not have to be written as ax^2+bx+c=0 to be a
quadratic. Naming a repeated piece with one letter turns x^4+bx^2+c=0 into a quadratic in u,
and the same move works when the repeated piece is a binomial such as 2x+3 or x-6, or a
fraction such as 1/x or 2x/(x-1). Undoing the substitution is part of the problem, since a
value of u is not a solution: with u=x^2 a positive value gives two values of x, zero gives
one and a negative gives none, so the count of real solutions is read after the undo, never
before. Clearing denominators turns an equation with the variable under a fraction bar into a
quadratic, and any candidate that makes an original denominator zero is extraneous. Cancelling
a shared factor between a numerator and a denominator leaves the cancelled value excluded.
An equation in two letters is a quadratic in one of them with the other held constant, which
gives a ratio rather than a value. Choosing between factoring, substituting, clearing
denominators and 9.4's root facts. The honest limit, that substitution only helps when the
resulting quadratic factors.
