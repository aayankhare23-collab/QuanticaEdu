# Full audit, 2026-08-12

Reachability-first sweep of the whole product before ad spend. 12 dimensions, 93 findings put to an independent skeptic whose job was to refute them, 69 confirmed and 21 thrown out. 106 agents.

The organising rule, learned the hard way twice today: **presence is not reachability.** Every item below was traced from a real URL or a real button to the line, and reports whether it RUNS, not whether it exists.

## Headline

The two bugs you found today were the same shape: a line that existed and never ran. This audit traced every important behaviour from a real URL or a real button, and the good news is that the core learning product is solid. Lessons load, answers grade, Milo answers a real question in 2.2 seconds, every one of 3,251 content items has a verified answer, and the funnel events you care about (lesson_start, ViewContent, lesson_complete, Purchase, and the signup path you fixed this morning) genuinely fire from real entry points. What is not safe to ship against paid traffic is the money layer and one page. Your two Subscribe buttons reach live Stripe right now while PAYWALL_LIVE_AT is 2099, so a real card gets charged 14.99 a month for content the FAQ on the same page promises them free forever, with no self-serve cancellation and a Terms page that says there is one. Your privacy policy tells parents that nothing is shared beyond five named companies, while every word a child types to Milo is POSTed to a Cloudflare Worker and on to a model vendor, neither of which appears anywhere in that policy. And because the app is guest-first, the Exit, Done, Escape and Back controls all send a guest to a page that requires an account, which bounces them onto your sales page and ends the session. Those three, plus one broken checkout resume, are the evening's work. Everything else is high-value polish that can follow the first ad impression.

## Ship blockers

Fix before money is spent. Everything else can follow the first impression.

### 1. You are charging real cards for a product that is currently free, and nobody can cancel

**Where** `paths/landing.html:655 and :669 (the two Subscribe buttons), landing.html:2547 (PAYWALL_LIVE_AT = 2099), landing.html:2614 (startCheckout has no paywall guard), terms.html:182, functions/index.js (no portal function)`  
**Effort** an hour · **Owner** both

**Why it matters now.** Both Subscribe buttons on the page your ads land on reach live Stripe today. I probed createCheckoutSession and it answered 401, the correct response for a deployed callable, so the money path is armed. Meanwhile isAccessGranted returns true on its first line for everyone because the paywall is off, so a buyer pays 14.99 a month for content they already have, and the FAQ directly under the button promises anyone who signed up while Quantica was free keeps free access. There is no billing portal, no billing section in the Settings modal, and no createPortalSession anywhere, while terms.html:182 tells them to cancel through account settings. One purchase from an ad click is a chargeback plus a California auto-renewal complaint, and support@ becomes a manual cancellation desk.

**Fix.** Pick one and do it tonight. Either hide the two Subscribe buttons and the price cards and say early access is free, or add a paywallOn() guard at the top of startCheckout so checkout cannot open while charging is off. Both are hosting-only edits. Either way, rewrite terms.html:182 to say cancellation is by email to support@quanticaedu.com, and put that same sentence plus the address in the Settings modal at paths/home.html:1141 where a subscriber will actually look. Do not flip PAYWALL_LIVE_AT forward to make this consistent, because the checkout resume bug below means a logged-out visitor still cannot pay.

### 2. The privacy policy tells parents nothing is shared beyond five named companies, and every Milo message goes somewhere else

**Where** `privacy.html:202 (and :189, :220, :221) against landing.html:8075 and landing.html:8182`  
**Effort** minutes · **Owner** both

**Why it matters now.** landing.html:8182 is the only fetch() in the entire 750KB app, and it POSTs the student's typed message to https://yellow-fog-a0e8.srivishnu0713.workers.dev, a Cloudflare Worker on a personal workers.dev subdomain, which relays it to a model vendor. Neither Cloudflare nor any AI provider appears in the section 4 processor list, which closes with the sentence that apart from the services listed above you do not share personal information with anyone. Worse, section 7 twice reassures the reader about exactly this data, saying the Meta pixel does not receive conversations with Milo and Clarity never sees a tutor conversation, so a parent is told where it does not go and never where it does. Section 3 tells them anything typed into Milo reaches us, which a parent reads as reaching you and stopping. Your ads target parents of minors, and this is the one place a child types free text.

**Fix.** Add a bullet to privacy.html section 4 naming Cloudflare, Inc. as edge hosting for the tutor and the model provider by name, and add one sentence to section 1 saying tutor conversations are transmitted and processed off-site. That is a text edit and a hosting deploy. Separately, move the Worker off a personal workers.dev subdomain onto a Quantica-controlled domain, because section 4 is also the document a regulator reads to check who your processors are.

### 3. Every guest who exits a lesson is thrown out of the product onto your sales page, and Back will not bring them home

**Where** `paths/home.html:1177, driven by landing.html:6313-6314 (closeLesson, navExit), landing.html:2280 (Exit course), landing.html:2248 (the Done button on a practice set), landing.html:6329 (Escape), landing.html:6360 (browser Back at the top level)`  
**Effort** an hour · **Owner** claude-can-fix

**Why it matters now.** The whole ad funnel is guest-first by design. Someone clicks your ad, taps Start free, onboards, works lesson 1.1, and has no account because nothing ever asked for one. Then they press Exit course, or Done at the end of a fresh practice set, or Back twice. All of those assign to /paths/home.html, whose auth callback at line 1177 unconditionally redirects a signed-out visitor to /paths/landing.html. I reproduced it live: a guest clicking .cb-exit ended on the marketing page, and pressing Back bounced them forward to it again because home.html re-runs the redirect. The Done button is the worst of these, because it is not a leave gesture. It also injects two junk pageviews into GA4 and Meta per exit, one for a home.html nobody saw.

**Fix.** In landing.html, make _homeUrl, navExit and closeLesson (6312-6314), the practice-set close (2248) and the top-level popstate (6360) check firebase.auth().currentUser and, when there is none, call showDashboard() instead of navigating. That keeps the guest in the app and avoids a second full load of the 750KB page. Do not fix this by letting guests into paths/home.html; the in-app fix is smaller.
